# Rule C2 | Containment Zone Procedures

### General Hazardous Containment Zone Procedures (CTZ)

When entering any containment zone of a biohazard the following requirements must be met by personnel upon entering them:

- Personnel has full or partial immunity
- Non-combatant escorted by at least 2 non-immune combatants or 1 immune combatant
- No active or unrestrained specimens are currently inside the containment chamber

If the risk involved is deemed too great by escorting security staff, non-combatants may be prohibited from entering the containment zone.

Only Level-3 Personnel may enter the Containment Zone (CTZ). RSD, U&M, Combatant Personnel, EC, ISB, and MD(VP & PB) may bypass the clearance level restriction to enter the CTZ/CISCZ if it's required to fulfill their duties.

### **CISCZ / Unit 3 Procedures**

The CISCZ and Unit 3 extension house mostly docile infected specimens that are researched by the facility and must be handled with extra caution. The following rules apply to the CISCZ and by extension the Unit 3 area when CIS are present:

- Non-combatants may only reside on the top floor of the CISCZ if there is at least one combatant present in the CISCZ and must leave if there are none present. They may enter to determine if there is a combatant present, but are not allowed to loiter around any longer and are to stay behind the yellow line.
  - To determine where you cannot go, there will be signs above doors that will say something along the lines of “biohazard zone ahead” with red lettering.
- Combatants with full/partial immunity may enter the bottom floor of the CISCZ freely.
- Non-combatants may only enter the bottom floor of the CISCZ if they have permission from the combatant personnel present and with 1 partially/fully immune combatant escorting them.
- CIP and immune non-combatants may enter the bottom floor of the CISCZ freely.
- Non-immune combatants are only allowed on the bottom floor of the CISCZ if they are given explicit permission by Recontainment Unit members with valid reasoning, or any other immune combatants if they are absent. If there are no immunes present, they may enter the bottom floor if it is necessary to do their duties with good reason (Hostiles roaming the bottom floor, sweeping for escaped inmates...).

### Builds in the CISCZ

In order to have decorative & harmless builds not be needlessly destroyed, buildings in the CISCZ are protected by the CoE whether they be built by CIS or TS and may **only** be destroyed if they’re disrupting combative duties; Being used as cover, obstructing important sightlines etc.

Combatant personnel reserve the right to tell any non-combatant personnel to leave the CISCZ or Unit 3 if they are acting in a disruptive manner or if there is any other danger imminent. RECON holds the highest authority over the CISCZ and Unit 3 when CIS are present during all codes below blue. This may change depending on the situation and they are to refer to the Security Department Chain of Authority in this scenario.

### Test Subject Containment Zone Procedures (TSCZ)

The TSCZ is a place where you will find most of the usually volatile Test Subjects. It starts from the break room exit to the inner TSCZ and goes all through up until the exit to the pool locker room which is located past the bathrooms. Due to the containment zone's erratic inmates, personnel should not enter the TSCZ unless they match one of the following:

- A combatant
- Intelligence personnel (EC, ISB)
- Personnel who have a clearance of Level-2 or higher
- Personnel who have a clearance of Level-1 which are being supervised by another individual which possesses a clearance of Level-3 or higher for the purpose of doing departmental duties.

Non-combatant personnel MUST stay behind the red combatant line unless they have a valid reason to pass.

- Valid reasons to cross include; U&M duties, MD personnel actively assisting combative personnel, etc.
- Escorted by 2 combatant personnel OR have designated VIP protection.
  - Designated VIP protection is considered any **regulated protection** such as JU, SO or ISB protection.
  - Semi-combatives **do** count as “combatant personnel”.

Personnel who do cross the red line (with one of the clauses stated above) are doing so at their own risk.

CIP should not pass the red line during an active breach due to a high chance of being misidentified or injured from anti-bio hazard tools. Under no circumstances can a CIP pass the red line during an active breach of Specimen-239.
